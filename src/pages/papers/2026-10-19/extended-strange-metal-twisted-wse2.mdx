## 30-second takeaway

In **twisted bilayer WSe2**, changing twist angle, hole filling and displacement field reveals an **extended strange metal** with nearly linear temperature-dependent resistance over a broad range. The authors find a **second superconducting dome** near filling 0.55, separate from the Mott-proximate dome near filling 1 and the van Hove singularity near 0.78. This challenges an explanation relying only on a narrowly tuned quantum critical point, without identifying a unique microscopic strange-metal mechanism.

## Big picture: why partial Hubbard-band filling matters

**Before:** moiré-TMD Hubbard studies emphasized the Mott state near one hole per effective moiré site and superconductivity adjacent to it. **Here:** correlated phases persist far away from this filling. **Consequence:** low-energy transport and pairing may depend on correlations across a broad portion of the band.

**AI-authored explanatory flow (conceptual, not a paper figure):**

```text
twist angle + gate-controlled filling
                 |
         band width / U and occupation
                 |
    +------------+---------------------+
    |                                  |
near filling 1                    near filling 0.55
Mott region → SC1                extended strange metal → SC2
                 |
      separate vHS near filling ~0.78
```

## Core mechanism / formula

The resistance analysis considers a low-temperature form

```text
rho_xx(T) = rho_0 + A * T^alpha

Fermi-liquid-like: alpha ~ 2
Strange-metal-like: alpha ~ 1
```

Extracting alpha is a **diagnostic**; T-linear resistance alone does not specify the microscopic scattering process. Experimental symmetry breaking and superconductivity are separate observables. In particular, low zero-field resistance, finite critical current and low-field suppression are compared instead of treating one resistivity minimum as conclusive superconducting proof.

## Authors report vs AI interpretation

### Authors report

The experiments show an extended strange-metal region for smaller twist angles, an SC2 dome far from the Mott filling and van Hove singularity, and a competing ferromagnetic insulating state near half filling. The authors argue the observations favor intrinsic correlated strange-metal physics.

### AI interpretation

This is a useful example of **mechanism discrimination by multidimensional transport controls**: filling, displacement field, twist angle, temperature and weak magnetic field. The new SC2 dome constrains theories that attach superconductivity exclusively to doping a weak Mott insulator.

### Caveats

The analysis does **not** determine pairing symmetry, directly measure a strange-metal scattering spectrum or exclude every quantum critical scenario. The phase boundaries near crossover regimes are not sharp thermodynamic transitions. Devices sample a finite range of twist angles. The manuscript is a preprint, not an independently reproduced result.
