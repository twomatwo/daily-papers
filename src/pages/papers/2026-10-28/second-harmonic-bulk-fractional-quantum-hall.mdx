## 30-second takeaway

Ordinary quantum Hall transport emphasizes a **linear** Hall plateau. Can the bulk of a **fractional** quantum Hall liquid produce a measurable **second harmonic** when inversion and rotational symmetries are broken, without invoking nonlinear edge-mode dispersion? Pimlott and colleagues derive a positive answer in an analytically controlled *model*: project a tilted massive Dirac band, retain the induced nonlinear electromagnetic coupling, and calculate the response with composite-boson Ginzburg–Landau–Chern–Simons (GLCS) theory. The second-harmonic **Hall resistivity** vanishes at zero frequency; its low-frequency real part scales as frequency squared, the imaginary part as frequency, and the finite-frequency filling dependence is proportional to **1/nu squared** under the stated plateau assumptions. This is a **theoretical prediction**, not an experimental observation or universal quantization of nonlinear response.

## Big Picture: a new bulk probe, not a replacement for Hall quantization

**Before:** the integer and fractional Hall effects are characterized by quantized *linear* transverse response. Nonlinear signals can also arise from Berry geometry, crystallographic asymmetry, and edge dynamics, but these mechanisms should not be conflated.

**This paper:** a specific **bulk** model produces second-harmonic transport even when nonlinear edge dispersion is not invoked. The result is sensitive to *wavefunction projection* and *fractional topological order*.

**After:** a future finite-frequency lock-in transport experiment could search for the predicted phase-sensitive second-harmonic voltage and angular dependence, while separately ruling out contacts, heating, and ordinary device nonlinearities.

## Mechanism I — the observable is resistivity, not conductivity

Drive a current at frequency omega and measure a transverse electric field at 2 omega. The authors write a nonlinear current response and **invert it order by order**. In their longitudinal/transverse basis (paper Eq. 2):

```text
rho_TLL^(2)(2 omega)
  = - rho_Ta(2 omega)
      sigma_abc^(2)(2 omega; omega, omega)
      rho_bL(omega) rho_cL(omega)
```

Repeated tensor indices are summed. All **three factors** of linear resistivity matter; substituting sigma_TLL^(2) alone would give the wrong observable and may misidentify resonances. A Hall plateau has approximately

```text
rho_H ~= h / (nu e^2)
sigma^(2) scales with nu  [within this theory]

therefore rho_TLL^(2) scales with nu^(-2)
```

This filling dependence is *not* a statement that the second harmonic is exactly quantized in arbitrary materials.

## Mechanism II — finite-frequency scaling and a cancellation

Let X be omega/omega_c, with omega_c the cyclotron frequency. In the large-gap, weak-tilt, low-frequency limit, the reported structure is (paper Eq. 4 and Supplemental Eq. S92):

```text
rho_TLL^(2)(2 omega, theta)
       ~= [h^2 / (nu^2 e^3)]
           { X^2 f(theta) + i X g(theta) }

Re rho^(2) ~ omega^2
Im rho^(2) ~ omega
rho^(2) -> 0 as omega -> 0
```

The functions f and g depend on band gap, Dirac velocity, valley, tilt and orientation; their prefactors are **not universal**. Their leading angular dependences are cosine-like and sine-like in the weak-tilt expansion. Compare the real and imaginary signal as well as the orientation of the Hall bar relative to the tilt axis.

**Kohn-mode subtlety:** the authors find poles corresponding to cyclotron/Kohn resonances in the *nonlinear conductivity*, but the product with the three linear resistivities cancels those poles in the *nonlinear resistivity* within the controlled regime. Remaining formal high-frequency poles lie beyond the effective single-band description. It would be misleading to predict a sharp 2-omega resistivity peak at the naively expected conductivity resonance.

## Authors report

- A **projected tilted massive Dirac model** produces nonlinear electromagnetic terms retained in an effective GLCS action for Laughlin-type fillings.
- Its computed second-harmonic Hall **resistivity** shows low-frequency omega-squared (real) and omega (imaginary) behavior, crystalline anisotropy and a nu-inverse-square scaling.
- Resonance poles in a second-order *conductivity* need not appear in the corresponding second-order *resistivity*.

## AI interpretation

This is a useful new observable for comparing *bulk fractional Hall response* and *edge-based nonlinear scenarios*. More broadly, it is an excellent lesson for second-harmonic transport: **the experiment fixes which quantity is driven and which is read out**, so converting sigma to rho must be done at nonlinear order. In bilayer/multiband devices, checking tensor inversion and symmetries can matter as much as fitting a power law.

## Caveats / discrimination experiments

- All central results are **theoretical**, obtained in a particular tilted, gapped, approximately single-band model and a perturbative mean-field GLCS treatment.
- Control of the band projection assumes drive energies and cyclotron energies well below the electronic gap; strong tilt and high drive frequencies fall outside the controlled approximation.
- Filling-factor **plateau width** is not derived: outside the exact filling, the authors assume extra carriers become localized quasiparticles while the bulk fluid remains intact. Disorder and localization need independent treatment.
- A second-harmonic Hall signal alone cannot prove fractional topological order; contacts, rectification, thermal signals and non-fractional Berry-geometry mechanisms must be discriminated.
- The full arXiv v1 HTML main text and relevant Supplemental sections were inspected, but numerical/field-theory algebra was **not independently reproduced**.
