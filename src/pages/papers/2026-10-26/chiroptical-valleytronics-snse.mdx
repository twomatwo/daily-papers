## 30-second takeaway

**The measured result:** SnSe, despite lacking the familiar hexagonal TMD valley structure, exhibits **opposite valley carrier populations under opposite helicities** of optical pump pulses. Time-resolved XUV photoemission measures an initially **−4.6 ± 0.3%** imbalance and an approximately **603 ± 78 fs** relaxation time (95% fit confidence bounds). **The predicted result:** ab initio dynamical calculations connect this asymmetric momentum population to a short-lived, helicity-switchable photocurrent **in an individual layer**. Neither a photocurrent nor a net bulk current was directly measured; adjacent bulk layers cancel the ideal signal.

## Big Picture: valley optics beyond hexagonal monolayers

**Before:** opposite Berry curvature near K/K-prime valleys of monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenides enables helicity-selective optical transitions. But non-hexagonal lattice symmetry changes which optical responses are allowed. **Here:** SnSe supports opposite-momentum valleys X/X-prime, and the photon helicity changes their populations. **What changes:** momentum microscopy can directly see a hidden layer-sensitive nonequilibrium valley response, while a net bulk transport response can vanish by symmetry.

## Mechanism and equations

Define left/right circular transition amplitudes at wavevector k as P_plus and P_minus. The normalized circular dichroism is

```text
eta(k) = (|P_plus(k)|^2 - |P_minus(k)|^2)
         / (|P_plus(k)|^2 + |P_minus(k)|^2)

       = -Omega_xy,vc(k) / [g_xx,vc(k) + g_yy,vc(k)]
```

Here the interband Berry curvature Omega is the antisymmetric/imaginary part of the interband quantum geometric tensor, and the metric g is its real/symmetric part. The positive-semidefinite metric implies |eta| ≤ 1; crystal symmetry can make the bound unattainable. That is why **optically visible nonzero polarization need not be nearly 100% valley-selective** as in some hexagonal TMDs.

For the current *estimated in theory*, the authors sum occupied band velocities:

```text
j(t) ∝ sum_(band n, k) f_nk(t) * v_nk
v_nk = (1 / hbar) grad_k epsilon_nk
```

The optical pump shifts valley populations. Since opposite momenta can have opposite group velocities, the population imbalance creates an allowed **layer-level** directional current along zigzag in the model. The ideal bulk's symmetry-related neighboring layers contribute opposing signals, so **the measurement of a surface-sensitive momentum imbalance is not equivalent to a net bulk current**.

## Authors report, interpretation, and caveats

### Authors report

Time- and angle-resolved photoemission verifies helicity-switchable momentum-space population imbalance in SnSe. Fits give a sub-picosecond relaxation time. Ab initio time-dependent Boltzmann simulations support quantum-geometric optical selection rules and predict a transient directional photocurrent in individual layers. The simulations relate relaxation to electron–phonon intervalley scattering.

### AI interpretation

The scientifically useful achievement is a **triangulation of symmetry, spectroscopy and microscopic simulation**. A natural next test is an independent electrical or ultrafast emitted-radiation measurement in a sufficiently symmetry-broken layer/few-layer sample. Such a result would independently test the predicted photocurrent, beyond measuring valley populations alone.

### Caveats

- **Measured:** photoexcited valley occupations and their lifetime. **Not measured:** an electrical photocurrent.
- Surface sensitivity and symmetry permit layer-local imbalances even though ideal bulk currents cancel.
- The computed decay is faster than the experiment in the discussed approximation; exact phonon-mode attribution depends on dynamics modeling.
- Optical dichroism reflects a transition matrix element and crystal symmetries; it is not a direct standalone measurement of arbitrary Berry curvature at every k.
- The preprint's reported fits were checked against text and figure captions; **no experiment or simulation independently reproduced**.
