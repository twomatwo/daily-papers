## 30-second takeaway

**The central finding:** rankings of universal machine-learned interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) can change dramatically when benchmark labels, reference-energy conventions, and geometry are carefully controlled. The authors score **14 models** on **1.45 million ordered intermetallics** against independent **AFLOW** DFT, then compare to Alexandria and OQMD references. Typical cross-DFT energy disagreement is roughly **6–8 meV/atom** (median), while heavy tails make average differences larger. Once **per-element energy offsets** and **relaxation drift** are controlled, the apparent leading trio's formation-energy errors cluster within **0.7 meV/atom** in the authors' Fig. 3 analysis. This is not evidence that every model is equally accurate or that DFT disagreement measures error relative to nature.

## Big Picture: benchmark the reference itself

A leaderboard frequently says that model A has lower formation-energy mean absolute error than model B **relative to a single DFT database**. But what if databases differ in pseudopotentials, workflows, relaxation geometry, and elemental reference energy? Measuring both models against only one reference may reward a matching convention rather than a more faithful potential-energy surface.

## Method: reference, geometry, and what an energy error means

A formation energy can be written conceptually as

```text
E_form(compound) = E(compound) - sum_i n_i * mu_i(element)
```

If a model uses different elemental reference values `mu_i` from the benchmark's DFT source, every compound containing element i receives a systematic offset even if the interatomic force law is otherwise good. For two **polymorphs of identical composition**, the elemental offsets cancel:

```text
Delta E_polymorph = E(structure A) - E(structure B)
```

The authors run model energies at two geometries: AFLOW reference structures (single-point) and the model's own relaxed structures. Comparing the two isolates **relaxation drift**; independently adjusting an elemental zero-point convention (Rung-0 control) isolates a **reference offset**. Neither operation retrains network weights. These controls separate the model's structure-dependent errors from bookkeeping and geometry-dependent factors.

### The measured cross-DFT "noise floor" is a resolution diagnostic

AFLOW, Alexandria and OQMD differ because of protocols and approximations; matching the same structures gives typical reference differences of about **6–8 meV/atom**. A minority of large differences push the mean to roughly **16–30 meV/atom**. These are **DFT-versus-DFT disagreements**, not statistical uncertainty of a laboratory measurement or an immutable bound on true physical accuracy. Even a model may appear closer to AFLOW than a second DFT database; that does not prove its energy is closer to experiment.

## Authors report, AI interpretation, and caveats

### Authors report

The authors benchmark 14 universal MLIPs using 1.45 million AFLOW intermetallics from a reference outside the studied models' stated training corpora. DFT databases show non-negligible inter-protocol discrepancies. Controls for geometry and elemental reference explain part of some model ranking gaps, but not all remaining errors. They report differing orderings on several properties and demonstrate how the training corpus shapes which uMLIPs generalize.

### AI interpretation

This is a **measurement theory paper for ML benchmarks** as much as a materials-model comparison. For any AI-for-science workflow, first quantify independent-label disagreement, identify symmetries/offsets that should cancel, check provenance, then evaluate properties that matter for downstream science. If an autonomous materials agent optimizes only one held-out formation-energy MAE, it can preferentially learn a DFT convention rather than the stability or forces relevant to an actual experiment.

### Caveats

- The benchmark is for **ordered intermetallic structures** and specified training/data protocols; performance rankings do not generalize to every alloy, oxide, defect, phase, or temperature.
- AFLOW is an independent **computational reference**, not experimental truth.
- The model's performance on energy, force, elastic response, lattice dynamics and hull classification is not interchangeable.
- Comparing a single-point energy at a fixed structure with a fully relaxed structure changes the physical question and must be stated clearly.
- Differences below the cross-DFT discrepancy scale need caution; they are not automatically statistically or physically distinguishable as improvements in the potential energy surface.
- The body, methods narrative, key figure captions and discussion were checked. **No benchmark computation rerun**.
