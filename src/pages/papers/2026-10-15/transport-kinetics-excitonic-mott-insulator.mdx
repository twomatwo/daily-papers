## 30-second takeaway

A high-mobility MoSe2/WSe2 interlayer-exciton cloud initially expands and then contracts when optically excited to higher densities. The authors propose occupation-induced Mott localization. Importantly, the observed PL response is a transport signature, not a direct gap or superfluid stiffness measurement.

## Mechanism

The drift–diffusion equation combines density gradients, repulsive interaction-driven drift, optical injection and decay. The authors' effective mobility decreases near unit moire occupation, inferred via an approximate mean-field PL blueshift. This assumed mobility profile reproduces cloud kinetics; it does not derive a Mott gap from first principles.

## Authors report versus AI interpretation

**Authors report:** non-monotonic exciton cloud width and central accumulation in a high-mobility sample; drift–diffusion simulations reproduce them.

**AI interpretation:** the low-density transport regime acts as an important internal control against simple static disorder trapping.

**Caveats:** high mobility only suggests superfluid behavior, and a cloud contraction alone cannot uniquely establish a thermodynamic Mott phase. The density calibration and occupation-dependent mobility require further tests.
