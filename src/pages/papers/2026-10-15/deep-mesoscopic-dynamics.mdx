## 30-second summary

DeepMesoD learns a thermodynamically structured **mesoscopic field equation** from microscopic spin trajectories: free energy, nonlocal interaction kernel, and state-dependent dissipation tied to thermal noise. The authors validate it on Ising-Kac, a two-dimensional ferromagnet, and CrBr3 stacking faults, reporting 3–4 orders of magnitude faster simulation.

## Core mechanism / formula

The nonconserved field equation combines gradient flow and thermal noise:

```text
d(phi)/dt = -K * grad(F) + div(K)/(beta*Vc)
            + sqrt(2/(beta*Vc)) * B * noise
K = B * transpose(B)
```

The additional divergence term represents an Itô correction when dissipation depends on state. The learned nonlocal spatial kernel enters the free-energy functional.

## Authors report / AI interpretation / caveats

**Authors report:** Ising-Kac limit recovered; atomistic ferromagnet trajectories matched through about 1 ns; a different initial state remains bounded for 25 ns; CrBr3 inferred stacking-fault-dependent interlayer coupling.

**AI interpretation:** learning an interpretable physical functional and operator can improve scientific validation relative to black-box rollouts.

**Caveats:** remaining bounded at 25 ns does not demonstrate 25 ns quantitative agreement. An assumed Markovian coarse closure can miss unresolved memory effects. Stacking assignments need independent structural tests.
